    Argentina: INA (Alerta5)

    Documentation index · Usage
    Provider ar_ina
    Country Argentina (plus one binational Río Uruguay border gauge in Uruguay)
    Published by Instituto Nacional del Agua (INA), Subgerencia de Sistemas de Información y Alerta Hidrológico (SIyAH), with federated national, binational, provincial and municipal networks
    Read from Official INA Alerta5 REST API (alerta.ina.gob.ar/a5)
    Quantities Discharge, stage
    Stations in the catalogue 829 river gauges. Availability depends on station, quantity, series and period
    Station metadata Station name and water-body name. See Fields by provider
    Credentials None
    Licence stated by Argentina.gob.ar Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (Creative Commons Atribución 4.0 Internacional, CC BY 4.0); source attribution required
    Agency documentation INA Alerta Hidrológico, Alerta5 portal, Alerta5 OpenAPI specification, Punctual series guide

    Retrieve four days of daily mean discharge at station 8, Andresito on the Iguazú River:

    import rivretrieve as rr

selection = rr.find(
    provider="ar_ina",
    station="8",
    quantity="discharge",
    frequency="daily",
    statistic="mean",
)

result = rr.fetch(selection, start="2024-01-01", end="2024-01-04", cache="bypass")

preview = result.data.select("time", "time_zone", "value", "unit").head(3)
print(preview.write_csv(), end="")

print(result.data.height)
print([(issue.severity, issue.code) for issue in result.issues])

    Output:

    time,time_zone,value,unit
2024-01-01T03:00:00.000000,+00:00,839.95,m3/s
2024-01-02T03:00:00.000000,+00:00,898.63,m3/s
2024-01-03T03:00:00.000000,+00:00,2051.27,m3/s
4
[('info', 'provenance.citation_not_established')]

    The request returned four published daily means in m³/s. The preview shows the first three days. Each Argentine calendar day since March 2009 begins at local midnight in UTC-03:00, so 1 January's daily mean is labelled 2024-01-01T03:00:00.000000 with time_zone="+00:00"; see Time and data status for historical Daylight Saving Time periods.

    The informational issue provenance.citation_not_established means the packaged provider catalogue has no fixed verbatim citation string configured. It does not mean retrieval failed or that attribution is optional; follow the attribution requirements in Terms and citation.

    cache="bypass", the default for live rr.fetch calls, requests Alerta5 directly without reading or writing a local cache. Passing cache="reuse" stores retrieved observations locally so later requests for already-covered dates reuse the local store. This output was checked live on 2026-11-09. Source records can be updated or revised over time. See Usage for selection options and working with returned data.

    Who measures, and who publishes

    Argentina's National Water Institute (Instituto Nacional del Agua, INA), through its Hydrological Information and Warning Systems Sub-directorate (Subgerencia de Sistemas de Información y Alerta Hidrológico, SIyAH), publishes national and Cuenca del Plata river observations through the Alerta5 service. Alerta5 aggregates gauge measurements collected by several operating networks, so appearance on Alerta5 does not mean that INA operates every station itself. The 829 river gauges in RivRetrieve's packaged catalogue belong to nine source networks:

    Source network in Alerta5 Stations
    Red Hidrológica Nacional, telemetric network (sat2, SSRH) 479
    Red Hidrológica Nacional, historical database (alturas_bdhi, SSRH) 235
    Prefectura Naval Argentina staff gauges (alturas_prefe) 88
    INA Delta and Greater Buenos Aires network (ina_delta) 16
    Salto Grande binational dam hydrometeorological network (estaciones_salto_grande) 4
    Nucleoeléctrica Argentina (na-sa) 3
    Municipality of Quilmes (quilmes) 2
    INTA Delta (inta_delta) 1
    Comisión Administradora del Río Uruguay (alturas_varios, station 1699 Nueva Palmira) 1

    No API key or account is required. RivRetrieve searches the packaged station and series catalogue offline; rr.fetch then requests JSON observations over HTTPS from Alerta5. Station identifiers are integer Alerta5 station IDs kept as strings, such as "8" or "1967". When the same physical river location has entries in more than one contributing network (for example a telemetric sat2 entry alongside a historical alturas_bdhi entry), Alerta5 assigns each network entry its own station ID, and RivRetrieve keeps them separate.

    The packaged ar_ina catalogue covers physical river and inland-delta gauges with observed discharge or stage records:

    • 828 gauges carry EPSG:4326 coordinates, and 1 river gauge ("96", Colorado on the Bermejo River) is retained with null latitude and longitude because Alerta5 publishes its stage series without station coordinates.
    • Inland Paraná Delta and freshwater inner Río de la Plata gauges (such as Tigre, San Fernando, Buenos Aires, and La Plata in alturas_prefe, and urban Greater Buenos Aires stream gauges in ina_delta) and the shared Río Uruguay border gauge 1699 (Nueva Palmira, Uruguay, operated by CARU) are included.
    • Virtual hydrological-model nodes, model simulations, forecasts, climatologies, and interpolated or derived analysis series stored in Alerta5 alongside gauge measurements are excluded; RivRetrieve admits only direct measurement (medición directa) and rating-curve (curva de gasto) observational procedures.
    • Maritime and outer-estuary coastal tide-gauge networks (Servicio de Hidrografía Naval estaciones_shn and PVNyMM estaciones_pvnymm), sparse manual field discharge gaugings (aforos), and groundwater phreatic wells are excluded.
    • Surface-water temperature has only two empty placeholder entries in Alerta5 with no observations, so ar_ina exposes discharge and stage only.

    Argentina's Subsecretaría de Recursos Hídricos (SSRH) also operates a separate federal hydrological portal (SNIH) with additional historical discharge, stage, and water-temperature records; a separate provider for SNIH is tracked in #729.

    What you can retrieve

    Alerta5 publishes five observational river discharge series types and eight observational river stage series types:

    Quantity filter Source field / variant Frequency Statistic Source unit Returned unit
    discharge Qmd (Caudal medio diario; per-series ID in variant) Daily Mean m^3/s m³/s
    discharge Q (Caudal; per-series ID in variant) Unknown Instantaneous m^3/s m³/s
    discharge Qmm (Caudal medio mensual; per-series ID in variant) Monthly Mean m^3/s m³/s
    discharge Qmmin (Caudal mínimo mensual; per-series ID in variant) Monthly Minimum m^3/s m³/s
    discharge Qmmax (Caudal máximo mensual; per-series ID in variant) Monthly Maximum m^3/s m³/s
    stage Hmd (Altura hidrométrica media diaria; per-series ID in variant) Daily Mean m m
    stage H (Altura hidrométrica; per-series ID in variant) Unknown Instantaneous m m
    stage H1h (Altura hidrométrica horaria; per-series ID in variant) Hourly Unknown m m
    stage Hm4h (Altura hidrométrica 4-horaria; per-series ID in variant) Unknown Unknown m m
    stage Hms (Altura hidrométrica media semanal; per-series ID in variant) Unknown Mean m m
    stage Hmm (Altura hidrométrica media mensual; per-series ID in variant) Monthly Mean m m
    stage Hmmin (Altura hidrométrica mínima mensual; per-series ID in variant) Monthly Minimum m m
    stage Hmmax (Altura hidrométrica máxima mensual; per-series ID in variant) Monthly Maximum m m

    Discharge in m^3/s and stage in m require no numerical scaling; RivRetrieve normalises the discharge unit label to m3/s while keeping "m^3/s" in source_unit. It does not calculate daily or monthly aggregates from sub-daily readings or merge separate series.

    Availability varies substantially across stations. In the packaged catalogue snapshot (2,611 witnessed station-series pairs across 829 gauges), instantaneous stage (H) is listed at 808 stations and daily mean stage (Hmd) at 756, whereas daily mean discharge (Qmd) is listed at 82 stations, instantaneous rating-curve discharge (Q) at 32, 4-hourly stage (Hm4h) at 391, hourly stage (H1h) at 3, and weekly mean stage (Hms) at 32. A station being listed in the catalogue does not guarantee observations for every quantity or requested period.

    Each published Alerta5 time series has its own numeric Alerta5 series ID, which RivRetrieve exposes as variant (for example variant="26605" for daily mean discharge at station "8"). Unlike providers with named processing levels, these numeric IDs belong to individual station series rather than forming a provider-wide menu. Within the packaged river catalogue, each station has at most one observational series per row in the table above.

    Several stage series have Unknown in Frequency or Statistic:

    • Hourly (H1h) and 4-hourly (Hm4h) stage: Alerta5 declares 1-hour and 4-hour time steps with a Continuous data type rather than Average. Comparing overlapping observations shows that these series place a single stage reading onto each 1-hour or 4-hour slot rather than averaging readings across the slot. RivRetrieve sets frequency="hourly" on H1h, leaves frequency unknown on Hm4h (4-hourly is not a named filter in RivRetrieve's frequency vocabulary), and leaves statistic unknown on both so mean and instantaneous filters do not select them by mistake.
    • Weekly mean stage (Hms): Summarises 7-day Monday-to-Monday intervals with statistic="mean". Because RivRetrieve's frequency filter vocabulary has daily and monthly rather than weekly, frequency remains unknown.
    • Instantaneous discharge (Q) and stage (H): Match statistic="instantaneous" without a frequency filter because telemetry and staff-gauge reporting intervals vary across stations and periods (from 10- or 15-minute automatic transmissions to daily or sub-daily manual readings).
    • Monthly minimum and maximum series (Qmmin, Qmmax, Hmmin, Hmmax): Report the minimum or maximum of the station's underlying observations within each calendar month, stamped at the start of the month.

    Inspect all candidate series and station metadata at a gauge offline before fetching, and use rr.pick(..., variant=...) (or frequency="hourly" for H1h) when targeting a series whose frequency or statistic is unknown:

    station_52 = rr.find(provider="ar_ina", station="52", quantity="stage")
candidates = rr.series(station_52)
metadata = rr.metadata(station_52)

hourly_stage = rr.pick(station_52, variant="31605")

    Stage references and station metadata

    River stage (altura hidrométrica) in Alerta5 is measured in metres relative to each station's local staff-gauge zero (cero de escala). RivRetrieve sets vertical_reference="gauge_zero" and leaves vertical_datum unknown on all stage series. Water levels below local gauge zero are physically valid during low-water periods and remain negative (for example -0.27 m in monthly minimum stage at station 8, Andresito). Do not compare raw stage values across different gauges as elevations above sea level.

    Station metadata from rr.metadata(selection) exposes two native fields from Alerta5:

    • nombre as station_name (populated for all 829 river gauges), and
    • rio in water_body_name_value (populated for 353 gauges and null at the remaining 476 gauges where Alerta5 leaves rio empty).

    Both drainage_area_* and elevation_* columns are null (state="no_metadata" in rr.metadata(selection, view="source")):

    • Alerta5's station inventory does not publish catchment areas, and its optional basin-polygon endpoint covers fewer than 3% of river gauges while mixing total drainage basins with incremental model subcatchments and zero placeholders.
    • An optional cero_ign attribute in Alerta5 (73 river gauges) records the height of the local staff-gauge zero relative to Argentina's Instituto Geográfico Nacional (IGN) datum rather than ground elevation, while a sparse altitud attribute (10 river gauges) states neither a unit nor a vertical datum. Neither field is exposed as station ground elevation.

    Time and data status

    Alerta5 returns all observation timestamps in UTC (...Z). RivRetrieve preserves those UTC timestamps with time_zone="+00:00" (time_zone="UTC" in rr.series(...)), and rr.to_utc(result) returns them unchanged.

    Pay attention to local civil midnight when aligning daily, weekly, or monthly aggregates with calendar dates:

    • March 2009 to present (UTC-03:00): Argentina has observed fixed UTC-03:00 without Daylight Saving Time since March 2009. Daily, weekly, and monthly intervals begin at 00:00 local time, which appears as 03:00:00 UTC (for example 2024-01-01T03:00:00.000000 for 1 January 2024).
    • Pre-2009 Daylight Saving Time transitions (UTC-02:00 in summer): During historical Argentine summer-time periods (such as October 2008 to March 2009), local midnight fell at 02:00:00 UTC (UTC-02:00). In Alerta5's database, the spring transition day spans 23 hours (03:00:00 UTC to the next day's 02:00:00 UTC), summer days begin at 02:00:00 UTC, and the autumn transition day spans 25 hours (02:00:00 UTC to 03:00:00 UTC). Because the UTC offset of local midnight shifts across historical DST years, RivRetrieve leaves day_definition unknown rather than claiming a fixed UTC offset across all decades.

    On daily, weekly, monthly, hourly, and 4-hourly series, time marks the start of the interval (timestamp_anchor="start"). On instantaneous discharge (Q) and stage (H), time marks the measurement instant (timestamp_anchor="measurement_time"), including exact sub-minute telemetry timestamps (such as :10 seconds) where recorded by automatic loggers.

    Alerta5 serves operational real-time telemetry and historical gauge archives through the same API without per-observation quality-assurance flags. It does publish a series-level operational-availability code (H, C, RT, NRT, S, and the H+S/C+S/RT+S/NRT+S combinations, or N for no observations available), documented on the a5-portal-home map. RivRetrieve keeps the default series frame unchanged and preserves that code as a series-scope annotation, under schema ar_ina.series:

    Field Type
    data_availability String

    Inspect it alongside the series it describes:

    print(rr.annotations(result).select("schema", "name", "dtype", "row_count").write_csv(), end="")

annotated = rr.series(result, annotations=True).select("series_id", "variant", "data_availability")
print(annotated.write_csv(), end="")

    Output:

    schema,name,dtype,row_count
ar_ina.series,data_availability,String,1
series_id,variant,data_availability
820d18989de6cb74eadc27b5686a24a8d09a051623953639e98272cd22e1fc4c,26605,NRT

    Station 8's daily mean discharge series (variant="26605") carries NRT (near-real-time) as its data_availability code. This output was checked live on 2026-11-17.

    This describes operational feed latency, not a data validation grade, and RivRetrieve makes no quality judgement of its own. RivRetrieve issues a targeted series-lookup request to harvest it even for a station already established from the packaged catalogue, so it is not tied to needing that lookup to resolve series identity. If that request fails, or the publisher's response omits the code, the annotation is simply absent for that series and an informational source.annotation_unavailable issue is recorded; this never blocks or invalidates the observation rows themselves.

    Keep missing-data cases distinct when inspecting results:

    • Missing dates or sub-daily gaps in Alerta5 generally produce no observation rows rather than null placeholders. RivRetrieve requests stored database observations only (suppressing Alerta5's synthetic regular-grid boundary nulls) and does not fill missing time steps.
    • Where Alerta5 stores an explicit database record whose value is null, RivRetrieve omits that gap marker and records an informational source.gap_marker issue in result.issues.
    • Long retrieval windows are split automatically into bounded requests (at most 365 days per request for sub-daily and slotted stage or instantaneous discharge, and at most 3,650 days per request for daily and weekly series) and assembled without boundary duplicates. Check issues whenever a request returns fewer rows than expected.

    Terms and citation

    INA's Alerta Hidrológico portal is published on Argentina's national argentina.gob.ar platform, whose terms and conditions license public content under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) (Creative Commons Atribución 4.0 Internacional) unless otherwise stated. Reuse requires attribution to the source.

    Both rr.describe("ar_ina") and result.provenance expose license="Creative Commons Atribución 4.0 Internacional", while citation is omitted from rr.describe("ar_ina") and None on result.provenance (because argentina.gob.ar states general CC BY 4.0 attribution obligations rather than prescribing a single verbatim citation string, which also produces the informational provenance.citation_not_established issue during rr.fetch). When publishing or redistributing retrieved data, credit the Instituto Nacional del Agua (INA), Subgerencia de Sistemas de Información y Alerta Hidrológico (https://alerta.ina.gob.ar/a5), name the contributing monitoring network where applicable (such as Subsecretaría de Recursos Hídricos, Prefectura Naval Argentina, CARU, or Salto Grande), and record the station ID, series variant ID, requested period, and retrieval date.

    RivRetrieve paces live requests politely (at most two requests per second) and reuses locally cached observations when cache="reuse" is passed.
